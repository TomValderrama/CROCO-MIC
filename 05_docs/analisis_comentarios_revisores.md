### R2-C1: ¿Los remolinos son submesoscala o mesoscala? [CRÍTICO]
**Cita literal:** *"The focus on this study is mesoscale eddies in the Gulf of Ancud. But I feel
that the eddies (shown in Figure 1a,12) could fall in the submesoscale regime since their
diameters are 10-30 km. Submesoscale eddies are generally affected by dynamics different
from mesoscale, e.g., mixed layer instabilities, and would change the physical explanations of
the results. Do the authors agree that the eddies they detect could fall in the submesoscale
range?"*

**Por qué es el comentario más importante:**
Si los eddies son submesoscala, toda la interpretación física del paper cambia. Los mecanismos
de generación (baroclinic instability → mesoscale) vs. (mixed layer instability → submesoscale)
son fundamentalmente distintos. El paper necesita resolver esto con números.

**Literatura de soporte:**

| Aspecto | Referencia | Argumento |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Definición submesoscala | McWilliams (2016) Proc. R. Soc. A | Submesoscala: Ro~O(1), escalas 0.1–10 km, MLI dominante |
| Definición mesoscala | Chelton et al. (2011) Prog. Oceanogr. | Mesoscala: Ro<<1, cuasi-geostrófico, Rd comparable |
| Shelf seas: ambos regímenes posibles | Badin et al. (2009) JGR | En shelf seas, eddies de 2–10 km pueden ser mesoscala |
| Radio de Rossby barotrópico | Chelton et al. (1998) JPO | Para 42°S: Rd ≈ 15–30 km → eddies ~comparables a Rd |
| Sub-superficie = menos influencia MLI | McWilliams (2016) | MLI dominante solo en capa de mezcla superficial |

**Argumento cuantitativo a construir:**

Para 42°S:
- f = 2 × 7.27×10⁻⁵ × sin(42°) = 9.72×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹
- Rossby radius Rd (1er modo baroclínico): estimar de perfiles CTD del MIC
  - Usando H_mixión ≈ 100–200 m, N ≈ 5×10⁻³ s⁻¹ → Rd = NH/f ≈ 5–10 km (baroclínico)
  - O usando datos de Chelton et al. (1998) para esta latitud: Rd ≈ 10–25 km
- U típico de los eddies: 0.1–0.3 m/s (de resultados del modelo)
- L (radio del eddy): 5–15 km
- **Ro = U/(fL) = 0.2 / (9.72×10⁻⁵ × 10×10³) = 0.2 / 0.97 ≈ 0.21**
  - Para L=15 km: Ro ≈ 0.14

**Conclusión:** Ro ≈ 0.1–0.2 → **régimen cuasi-geostrófico (mesoscala)**, no submesoscala.
Además, los cores de los eddies son sub-superficiales (50–200 m), por debajo de la capa
de mezcla (< 50 m en verano austral), lo que elimina MLI como mecanismo principal.

**Texto para carta de respuesta:**
> "We thank the reviewer for raising this important dynamical question. We have added a new
> paragraph in Section 1 (Introduction) that explicitly addresses the mesoscale vs. submesoscale
> classification of the Gulf of Ancud eddies. Computing the Rossby number Ro = U/fL for the
> identified eddies, using model velocities U ~ 0.1–0.3 m s⁻¹, eddy radii L ~ 5–15 km, and
> f = 9.72×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ at 42°S, we obtain Ro ~ 0.1–0.2, consistent with a quasi-geostrophic
> mesoscale regime (Ro << 1) following McWilliams (2016) and Chelton et al. (2011). Furthermore,
> the observed eddies are sub-surface intensified, with their cores located at depths of 50–200 m,
> well below the summer mixed layer depth (~30–50 m). This depth range precludes mixed layer
> instabilities as the primary generation mechanism. The eddy diameters (10–30 km) are
> comparable to the estimated first baroclinic Rossby radius in the region (Rd ~ 10–25 km;
> Chelton et al., 1998), further supporting the mesoscale classification. We note that Badin
> et al. (2009) addressed an analogous question for shelf sea eddies of similar scales and
> concluded that the quasi-geostrophic framework is appropriate when Ro < 0.5. We have
> incorporated Ro = U/fL calculations for all identified eddies in Table X."

> topographic vorticity generation while preserving a stratification profile comparable to the

> who used a similar constant-depth configuration to isolate topographic effects from
> baroclinic generation in a comparable Chilean coastal setting."

### R2-C4: Duración de las simulaciones y robustez estadística
**Cita:** *"I am concerned that such short simulations may not be sufficient to yield statistically
robust results for the eddy count. As shown in Table 2, the number of eddies identified in each
experiment is only 2–4."*

**Literatura de soporte:**
- Chelton et al. (2011): incluso en estudios globales con miles de eddies, la robustez
  estadística del conteo es un tema activo. Para estudios regionales/proceso, se acepta n<10
  si el análisis es cualitativo.
- Contreras et al. (2019): identifican ~5–8 eddies en 6 meses de simulación → comparable
  a nuestro estudio.
- Dong et al. (2007): análisis de island wakes con n=3–5 eventos, usando análisis de presencia/ausencia.

**Argumento a hacer:**
1. Presencia/ausencia es más robusto estadísticamente que frecuencia exacta.
2. La consistencia entre verano e invierno (dos períodos independientes) refuerza el resultado.
3. El análisis de EKE (energía cinética de los remolinos) es menos sensible al conteo discreto.
4. Proponer simulaciones más largas como trabajo futuro.

**Texto para carta de respuesta:**
> "We agree that n=2–4 eddies per experiment limits the statistical precision of frequency
> estimates. We have addressed this in two ways: (1) we now focus our comparative analysis
> on qualitative eddy presence/absence and EKE differences between experiments rather than
> on point estimates of eddy count (following Dong et al., 2007; Contreras et al., 2019);
> (2) we explicitly state this limitation in the Discussion (Section X) and identify multi-year
> simulations as the primary avenue for future work. The consistency of results across two
> independent seasonal periods (summer and winter) provides additional confidence that the
> main conclusions are robust despite the limited sample size."
